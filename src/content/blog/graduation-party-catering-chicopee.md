Graduation season in the Pioneer Valley stacks up fast. Between Chicopee Comprehensive, Chicopee High, and the local colleges wrapping up in May and June, a lot of families are trying to book the same weekends. If you are hosting a grad party this spring or summer and you do not want to spend the morning of the party standing over a stove, our catering program is worth a serious look.

We have been feeding Chicopee families since 1993 out of our spot at 409 Broadway, and the catering side draws from the same kitchen we cook in every day.

## Pick a Package That Fits Your Headcount and Budget

The buffet packages are the most popular choice for graduation parties because they let guests serve themselves at their own pace, which is exactly what you want when people are arriving in waves between 1 p.m. and 4 p.m.

Five buffet packages cover most situations:

- **Buffet Package #1** and **Buffet Package #2** are both priced at $13.95 per person.
- **Buffet Package #3** and **Buffet Package #4** come in at $14.95 per person.
- **Buffet Package #5** steps up to $15.95 per person.

The per-person pricing makes it straightforward to estimate your total once you have a rough headcount. For a party of 30, for example, Buffet Package #3 would run you roughly $448.50.

If you want more variety on the hot side, the **Hot Item Buffet** is $15.95 per person and is built around warm dishes that hold well on a buffet line.

## Specialty Buffets for Something a Little Different

If the guest of honor has a preference, the specialty buffet options give you a bit more personality:

- **Choice Buffet #1** at $14.95 per person
- **Choice Buffet #2** at $17.95 per person
- **Italian Buffet** at $17.95 per person

The Italian Buffet works especially well for larger family gatherings where you want a spread that feels more like a sit-down meal even in a buffet format.

## Book Early, and Come In with a Rough Number

May and June are our busiest catering months for obvious reasons. If you know your party date, reach out as soon as possible. You do not need a final headcount to start a conversation, but having a rough range, say 20 to 25 guests versus 50 to 60 guests, helps us point you toward the right package.

We drop off and set up, but make sure you have enough table space for the buffet line and serving dishes before we arrive. A quick look at your space ahead of time avoids last-minute scrambles on the day.

## The Point of All of It

Graduation is one of those days where the food should be plentiful, taste good, and not require the host to miss the toast because they were stuck in the kitchen. That is the goal.
