## Not the Stuff from a Can

If you have spent any time at a diner, you know the version of hash that comes straight from a can, dumped on the griddle, and served as filler. That is not what we make. The Homemade Hash at The Copperline Eatery has been on our menu since we opened in 1993, and it is made in-house, which is exactly what the name says.

We list it plainly: **Homemade Hash (small)** and **Homemade Hash (large)**, sitting in the Breakfast Sides section of the menu. Regulars already know what it is.

## What Goes Into It

Our hash starts with a base of diced potatoes and seasoned meat, cooked on the flat-top until the edges get that browned, slightly crispy texture. The inside stays tender. It is not uniform, because it is not a processed product. You will get variation in each bite, which is a feature, not a flaw.

The large portion lands as its own satisfying plate. The small works well alongside eggs and toast, and a lot of people order it in place of homefries when they want something with a little more going on.

## How People Order It

The most straightforward is ordering it as a standalone side, small or large. But if you want it built into a full breakfast, the **Hash Breakfast** puts it front and center, pairing the hash with eggs and the usual accompaniments, all for $11.50. That plate has been a quiet workhorse on our menu for years.

There is also the **Hash Omelet**, one of our Specialty Omelets, which folds the hash directly into the egg. The combination works better than it might sound on paper. The hash adds substance and a different texture than you get from a standard vegetable or cheese filling.

## A Side That Stands on Its Own

Most breakfast sides exist to support the main event. Homefries, toast, a strip of bacon, they fill space on the plate and round out the meal. The hash is a little different. Plenty of people come in and build their whole order around it.

If you usually order **2 Eggs, Toast and Homefries** and have never swapped the homefries for hash, try it at least once. The two are priced and sized differently, so ask your server what makes sense for what you are hungry for.

## Thirty-Plus Years of the Same Recipe

We have been making the hash the same way since the early days on Broadway. There is no reason to reinvent it. Chicopee has seen a lot of restaurants come and go since 1993, and the ones that stick around usually found a few things worth doing well and kept doing them.

The hash is one of those things for us.
